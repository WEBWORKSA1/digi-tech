Digital technology is any tool that stores, processes or sends information as numbers. Your phone, your bank's app, a streaming service, a smart thermostat and the website you are reading are all examples. The word comes from *digit*: underneath, everything is counted.

## Digital versus analogue

An analogue signal is a continuous copy of the thing it represents. The groove in a vinyl record is the shape of the sound wave. A mercury thermometer rises smoothly with the temperature.

A digital signal measures that same thing at regular moments and writes each measurement down as a number. A music file records the height of the sound wave tens of thousands of times a second. A digital photo records the colour at each of millions of points.

That sounds like a small difference. It turns out to be enormous, for three reasons.

1. **Numbers copy perfectly.** Every copy of a tape is slightly worse than the original. Every copy of a file is identical.
2. **Numbers travel well.** A signal that picks up noise on its journey can be cleaned up at the other end, because the receiver only has to decide between a small set of possible values.
3. **Numbers can be calculated with.** Once a photo is numbers, software can sharpen it, search it, compress it or recognise a face in it.

## Why only ones and zeros

Computers store every number in binary, using just two digits. Each one or zero is a **bit**. Eight bits make a **byte**, which is enough to hold one letter of English text. Two states are used because they are easy to build and hard to confuse: a switch is on or off, a spot on a disc is magnetised one way or the other.

Everything else is agreement about what the numbers mean. One standard says the number 65 is the letter A. Another says three numbers in a row are the red, green and blue of one dot on a screen. Try it with the [binary and hex converter](../../tools/number-base/).

## The four jobs every digital device does

- **Input** turns the world into numbers: a keyboard, a camera sensor, a microphone, a touch screen.
- **Processing** follows instructions to change those numbers. This is the job of the processor, running software.
- **Storage** keeps numbers for later, on a drive or a memory chip.
- **Output and communication** turn numbers back into light and sound, or send them to another device over a network.

A smartwatch and a data centre differ in scale, not in kind.

## Where the internet fits

A network is two or more devices exchanging numbers. The internet is a network of networks that agreed on one set of rules for doing so. The web, email, video calls and apps are services that run on top of it. When a service runs on someone else's computers and you reach it over the internet, that is what people mean by the cloud.

## What it means for you

Understanding the basics pays off in ordinary decisions. Knowing that a megabit is not a megabyte tells you whether a broadband plan is fast enough, which the [internet speed guide](../internet-speed-explained/) covers. Knowing that copies are perfect and free explains why [backups](../backup-3-2-1/) are so cheap, and why leaked data can never be taken back. Knowing that software only follows instructions explains both its power and its mistakes.
