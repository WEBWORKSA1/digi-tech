Broadband is sold with one big number, and most confusion about it comes from a single letter. Here is what the numbers mean and how to tell whether you are getting what you pay for.

## Bits and bytes

A **bit** is a single one or zero. A **byte** is eight bits.

- Connection speeds are measured in **bits** per second: Mbps means megabits per second.
- File sizes are measured in **bytes**: MB means megabytes.

The lowercase b and the capital B are not interchangeable. To turn a speed into something you can compare with a file size, divide by eight.

| Plan speed | Downloads about | A 5 GB film takes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 25 Mbps | 3 MB a second | 27 minutes |
| 100 Mbps | 12.5 MB a second | 7 minutes |
| 500 Mbps | 62 MB a second | 80 seconds |
| 1 Gbps | 125 MB a second | 40 seconds |

For other sizes, use the [download time calculator](../../tools/download-time/).

## The three measurements on a speed test

**Download speed** is how fast data reaches you. It governs streaming, browsing and downloads, and it is the number in the advert.

**Upload speed** is how fast you send. It governs video calls, cloud backups and sending large files. On cable and many mobile connections it is a small fraction of the download speed. Full-fibre connections are often equal in both directions.

**Latency**, also called ping, is the delay before a response arrives, in milliseconds. It governs how responsive things feel. Under 30 ms is excellent, under 60 ms is fine for calls and games, and above 100 ms you notice people talking over each other. A fast connection with high latency can still feel sluggish.

## How much do you need?

As a guide for each thing happening at the same moment:

- Web, email and music: 1 to 2 Mbps
- HD video call: 3 to 4 Mbps, in both directions
- HD streaming: about 5 Mbps
- 4K streaming: 15 to 25 Mbps
- Online gaming: 3 to 6 Mbps, but low latency matters far more

Add up what your household does at its busiest and leave some headroom. For most homes of two to four people, 100 Mbps is comfortable. Higher tiers mainly shorten large downloads.

## Why you get less than the advert

- **Wi-Fi is usually the bottleneck.** Distance, walls and neighbours' networks all reduce it. Test with a cable to the router to see the true line speed.
- **The connection is shared.** Every device in your home takes a slice, and in the evening so do your neighbours on the wider network.
- **Advertised speeds are typical or maximum figures,** not guarantees. Check the small print for the minimum.
- **The other end has limits too.** A busy server sends only as fast as it can.

If a cable test is fine and Wi-Fi is not, work through [how to fix slow Wi-Fi](../fix-slow-wifi/).
