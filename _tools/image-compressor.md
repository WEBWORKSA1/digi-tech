## How to use it

Choose an image, pick the format to save in, and move the quality slider while watching the preview and the size figures. Set a maximum width to resize at the same time. When the balance looks right, download the result.

The work is done by your own browser. The image is never uploaded, which makes this safe for personal photos and client work.

## Settings that work

- For photos on a website, JPEG or WebP at 70 to 80 quality is usually indistinguishable from the original at a fraction of the size.
- Resize before anything else. A photo straight from a phone is often 4,000 pixels wide, and most web pages never show it wider than 1,600.
- Use PNG only for graphics with sharp edges, text or transparency. For photos it produces much larger files.

## Questions people ask

### Why do smaller images matter?

Images are usually the heaviest part of a web page. Smaller files load faster, use less mobile data and help a page score better on the speed measures search engines look at.

### Which format should I choose?

The [image formats guide](../../guides/image-formats/) compares JPEG, PNG, WebP and AVIF in detail.

### Does compressing remove location data?

Yes. Redrawing the image drops the hidden details stored by the camera, including GPS position.
